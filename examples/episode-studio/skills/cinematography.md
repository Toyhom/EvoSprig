Give each shot one dramatic purpose. Use wide shots to establish space, medium shots for interaction and close-ups for decisions or reactions. Alternate scale when it serves the story. Let dialogue breathe; reserve time for physical action and listening.

Prefer simple, legible blocking. State who is visible, where they are and what changes during the shot. Avoid multiple unrelated camera moves, abrupt costume changes or dense action within a few seconds. A slower shot can be useful when it creates contrast with the next action.

For 2D animation, ask for consistent linework, cel shading, expressive silhouettes and painted environments. Keep materials and highlights compatible with that style. Sound should have a clear foreground: one speaker or one important action sound, with ambience below it. Use silence and held poses where appropriate.
