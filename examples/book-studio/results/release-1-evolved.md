# Book studio — evolved

**Chapter 1: Know Your Real Number**

Your paycheck says $2400, but where does it actually go? Before planning, observe. For one month, track every purchase—coffee, rent, transit, that impulse buy.

Then sort spending into three buckets:
- **Fixed needs:** rent, utilities, loan payments
- **Flexible needs:** groceries, gas, phone
- **Wants:** dining out, streaming, clothes

Say your totals land at $1300 fixed, $500 flexible, $400 wants—that's $2200, leaving $200 unaccounted. Now you know your real number.

**Exercise:** Log every purchase for 30 days in a notebook or spreadsheet. At month's end, total each category. Compare the sum to $2400. The gap is your starting point.

**Chapter 2: Build a Zero-Based Plan**

A zero-based plan gives every dollar of your $2,400 a job before the month begins. Start with needs: rent $900, utilities $120, groceries $300, transport $150. That's $1,470. Next, pay yourself: move $300 to savings before you spend on fun. Now wants: $400 for dining out, streaming, and hobbies. That totals $2,170, leaving $230. Don't leave it floating—assign it. Add $100 to savings and keep $130 as a buffer for surprises like a co-pay or a friend's birthday gift. Income minus every assignment should equal zero.

**Exercise:** On paper, draft next month's plan using your real numbers. Adjust category amounts until $2,400 minus all assignments equals exactly zero.

**Chapter 3: Run a Monthly Check-In**

At month's end, compare your plan to reality. Pull your spending totals and line them up against your zero-based plan. Say you budgeted $300 for groceries but spent $380—that $80 gap is your signal, not a failure. Find the biggest gap first; small ones can wait.

Ask three questions: Where did I overspend most? What triggered it? What's one change for next month? Maybe you raise groceries to $340 and trim $40 from takeout. Adjust one thing, not everything.

**Exercise:** At month's end, write your three review questions and answers, then set one specific adjustment for next month's plan. Repeat monthly—your budget gets sharper each cycle.

This three-chapter onboarding guide introduces a recent graduate to household budgeting using a consistent fictional monthly income of $2,400. Each chapter is under 150 words and includes a practical exercise. The guide avoids recommending financial products and focuses on observation, planning, and monthly review.

## Reading Order
1. **Chapter 1: Know Your Real Number** — establishes baseline spending through tracking.
2. **Chapter 2: Build a Zero-Based Plan** — converts tracked spending into an assigned plan.
3. **Chapter 3: Run a Monthly Check-In** — closes the loop with review and adjustment.

The sequence is logical: observe first, then plan, then refine. No reordering needed.
