**Chapter 1: Define the Event and Lock the Budget**

Start with one clear goal: our fictional "Riverside Neighbor Cleanup" on Saturday, May 18, 9 a.m.–noon at Riverside Park, for 30 volunteers. Write the goal in a single sentence and share it with every helper.

Lock the $600 line-item budget before booking anything: venue permit $75, supplies (gloves, bags, grabbers) $200, refreshments $175, signage and printing $75, contingency $75. Book the pavilion and confirm it has an accessible path and restrooms.

Assign roles now: coordinator, volunteer check-in lead, supplies lead, refreshment lead, and safety lead.

For our 30-person neighborhood cleanup and picnic, recruit eight volunteers via a sign-up sheet at the community center and a group text. Assign roles: two setup, two refreshment servers, two cleanup leads, one check-in, one float. Schedule shifts of two hours so no one burns out. Spend $180 on supplies (gloves, bags, plates, cups), $150 on refreshments (sandwich platters, lemonade, fruit), and $40 on signage—keeping the $600 budget on track. Reserve the pavilion as a rain backup and confirm it holds 30.

# Editorial Handoff: Volunteer Community Event Guide

## Introduction
This three-chapter guide walks a first-time organizer through planning a small volunteer event, using a consistent fictional example: the "Riverside Neighbor Cleanup," a 30-person event at Riverside Park with a $600 budget. Each chapter ends with a practical checklist. The handoff below flags reading order, consistency fixes, and facts that need sourcing before publication.

## Reading Order
The chapters are numbered and should run in sequence: **Ch. 1 (Define the Event and Lock the Budget) → Ch. 2 (Recruit Volunteers and Plan Logistics) → Ch. 3 (Prepare Contingencies and Run the Event).** The logic is sound—goal and budget first, then recruitment and purchasing, then day-of execution. No reordering needed.

## Consistency Corrections
The example is mostly coherent, but several details conflict across chapters and should be reconciled:

1. **Budget totals don't match.** Ch. 1 sets supplies at $200 and refreshments at $175. Ch. 2 spends $180 on supplies and $150 on refreshments. Pick one set of figures and apply throughout. Note Ch. 2's "$370 total" ($180 + $150 + $40) also omits the venue permit and contingency from Ch. 1, so the running total should be restated against the full $600.
2. **Event name and format drift.** Ch. 1 and Ch. 3 call it the "Riverside Neighbor Cleanup"; Ch. 2 calls it a "cleanup and picnic." Standardize the name and decide whether the picnic/refreshment element is part of the event.
3. **End time conflicts.** Ch. 1 sets the event at 9 a.m.–noon. Ch. 3 runs the event 9:00–11:30 with teardown by noon. Align these (e.g., event 9:00–11:30, teardown noon) and update Ch. 1.
4. **Volunteer count vs. roles.** Ch. 1 assigns five named roles; Ch. 2 recruits eight volunteers into a different role set (two setup, two refreshment, two cleanup, one check-in, one float). Clarify whether the five Ch. 1 roles are leads drawn from the eight, and make the two lists consistent.
5. **Weather backup location differs.** Ch. 1 says "note indoor backup option"; Ch. 2 reserves the pavilion as rain backup; Ch. 3 moves to a community hall two blocks away. Choose one backup plan (pavilion vs. community hall) and use it in all three chapters.
6. **Accessibility details vary.** Ch. 1 confirms an accessible path and restrooms; Ch. 3 specifies an accessible portable toilet. Confirm which facility is actually available and state it once, consistently.
7. **Checklist formatting is inconsistent.** Ch. 1 uses `- [ ]`, Ch. 2 uses `☐`, Ch. 3 uses `☐`. Standardize to one checkbox style across all chapters.

## Facts Needing Sources
Before publication, verify or cite the following:

- **Permit requirement and cost ($75):** Confirm that Riverside Park (or the equivalent real venue) requires a permit for a 30-person cleanup and that the fee is accurate.
- **Pavilion/community hall capacity (30 people):** Verify stated capacity and bookability, including whether the hall is actually "two blocks away."
- **Accessibility claims:** Paved paths, accessible restrooms/portable toilet, and availability of large-print materials should be confirmed with the venue, not asserted.
- **Insurance/waivers:** The guide omits any mention of liability waivers or event insurance, which many parks require for volunteer cleanups—worth adding and sourcing.
- **Supply and refreshment pricing:** The line-item costs (gloves, grabbers, sandwich platters, etc.) should be checked against current vendor quotes so the $600 budget is realistic.
- **Communication-tree timing (7 a.m. calls):** Confirm this is a recommended practice rather than a stated requirement; if presented as best practice, cite a source.
- **Thank-you window (48 hours):** If framed as a standard, attribute it; otherwise present as a suggestion.

## Minor Notes
- Ch. 1's "share it with every helper" assumes helpers are already identified before recruitment in Ch. 2—consider softening to "share with your planning team."
- Add a one-line transition at the end of each chapter pointing to the next.
