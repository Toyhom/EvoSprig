# Decision Brief: AI Customer Support

**Recommendation:** Automate routine FAQ only, under staged gates; keep humans on all non-FAQ and sensitive transactions. Both sides converged here.

**Strongest case for support:** 80/100 FAQ accuracy on the dominant workload (~495 of 900 monthly tickets) justifies automating the routine majority, redeploying two staff to the 45% needing judgment. Net human load falls from 495 FAQ tickets to ~99 corrections if the correction queue stays below deflection.

**Strongest case against:** Zero evidence on 405 non-FAQ tickets/month; 20% FAQ error rate implies ~99 wrong answers monthly; escalation detection unmeasured; refunds/access require human approval, making "all support" incoherent.

**Disagreements and adjudication:**
1. *Pre-shadow 95% accuracy bar:* Evidence favors support — 95% is underived and blocks the shadow measurement that resolves the dispute. Use severity-weighted gates instead.
2. *Staffing infeasibility:* Evidence favors support — the objection is a comparison, not an absolute; correction queue must be measured, not assumed.
3. *Escalation detection at Gate 1:* Evidence favors challenge — a missed escalation is a silent failure; support conceded this.

**Stop rules (rollback to prior gate):**
- Misleading-answer rate > 1% over 2 consecutive weeks.
- Escalation-detection rate < 90% over any 2-week window.
- Correction queue > deflection volume over 4 consecutive weeks.
- Complaint rate > human baseline by 2 percentage points over 4 weeks.
- Any policy-risk error in a 200-ticket audit sample.

**Evidence gaps:**
- *Non-FAQ performance:* accepted risk — no autonomy without separate per-category pilot.
- *Human FAQ baseline:* mitigate by measuring baseline on matched tickets in week 1.
- *Failure severity of the 20 pilot errors:* mitigate by re-auditing all 20 in Gate 0.
- *Confidence interval (71–87%):* mitigate by gating on lower bound, not point estimate.
