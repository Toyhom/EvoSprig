## Text

# Decision Brief: Replacing Half the Study Desks with a Makerspace

## Bottom line

**Do not convert the desks now. Fund the $3,000 temporary-room pilot, with pre-registered go/no-go criteria and the accessibility review as a hard gate on any conversion or equipment installation.** Both the strongest challenge and the strongest support cases converge on this action; the disagreement is narrower than the framing suggests.

## The strongest arguments

**Against conversion (challenge case):**
1. **You'd cut the constrained resource, not the slack one.** Peak exam occupancy is 92%; a permanent desk removal bites hardest exactly then. Nothing in the context shows the library can absorb that loss at peak.
2. **The survey can't carry the decision.** 60 volunteers out of 4,000 (1.5%), self-selected, establishes *interest among the already-engaged* — not *demand at the cost of displaced study seats*. Both sides now concede this.
3. **Cost asymmetry favors sequencing.** $24,000 conversion vs. $3,000 pilot (8:1). Spending the larger sum before the reversible test inverts the logic of cheap experimentation.
4. **A known unknown blocks a responsible decision.** The accessibility review is incomplete, and desk removal touches a core accessibility-relevant service.

**For proceeding (support case):**
1. **There is a real, if weak, demand signal** — the only direct preference data available points toward support.
2. **Space is not uniformly scarce.** 38% non-exam occupancy means most of the year has slack; the constraint is temporal.
3. **The pilot is cheap and reversible** — it buys the evidence the $24,000 decision actually requires for one-eighth the cost.

## Where the cases converge (and the real disagreement)

Both rebuttals explicitly accept the same decision: **pilot first, convert only on evidence, accessibility review as a gate.** The genuine residual disagreements are narrow:

- **Label:** "Reject" vs. "defer conversion; approve pilot." The challenge case's own rebuttal concedes "reject" risks killing the pilot in governance. Use the defer label.
- **Pilot timing:** The support case originally proposed running *outside* exam weeks, then corrected itself — that would fail to test the exact peak-overlap risk. **Run the pilot through a term that includes exam weeks**, with peak occupancy as a measured criterion.
- **Accessibility gate scope:** Gate conversion and equipment install, not the $3,000 pilot funding (support's correction, accepted by challenge).

## Unresolved disagreements

- Whether 40 supportive volunteers is a meaningful prior or should be discarded entirely (both lean toward "weak prior only").
- Whether protecting 92% peak capacity is a factual constraint or an explicit value judgment about mission priority — it is the latter and should be stated as such.
- Whether the temporary room can host a *fair* test (space, power, noise isolation) — unestablished.

## Evidence gaps

1. **Representative demand data** — no random sample of the 4,000 members exists.
2. **Peak-overlap behavior** — unknown whether makerspace demand would collide with exam-period desk demand.
3. **Accessibility impact** — review incomplete; this is the binding unknown.
4. **Pilot feasibility** — whether a temporary room can serve as a valid test is unverified.
5. **Absorption capacity** — whether 38% non-exam slack could host makerspace use without touching peak desks is untested.

## Practical recommendation

**Decision: Defer conversion; approve the pilot.**

**Reversible next step:** Fund the $3,000 temporary-room pilot for one term, scheduled to **include exam weeks**, with **pre-registered, binding success criteria** set before data collection:
- Measured makerspace usage hours above an agreed threshold;
- Peak exam desk occupancy preserved at or above the current 92%;
- Member feedback captured;
- Accessibility review completed and clearing the design **before any equipment install or conversion**.

**Go/no-go:** Revisit the $24,000 conversion only if all three hold — usage exceeds threshold, peak occupancy is preserved, and accessibility review clears. If the temporary room proves unable to host a fair test, defer entirely until a valid test space exists rather than proceeding on the survey.

The pilot is a genuine test, not a stall — and it costs one-eighth as much to learn the same thing.
