# Decision Brief: E-Bike Subsidy vs. Bus Frequency

## Bottom line

Neither option is justified as a full-budget commitment on the current evidence. The strongest defensible move is a **staged, evidence-gated decision**: fund the bus frequency improvement now (its benefit is plausible and its constituency is identified), while running a **small, lane-gated bike pilot** to replace the vendor forecast with measured data. This is not a compromise for its own sake — it follows from where the two cases actually converge and where the evidence genuinely runs out.

---

## 1. Where the two cases converge (treat as settled)

Both the challenge and support cases, after rebuttal, agree on four points. These should anchor the decision:

1. **The vendor forecast (300 daily rides) cannot carry a fixed-budget commitment.** Both sides concede it is a sales claim, not a planning input. This is the single most important fact in the debate.
2. **Incomplete bike lanes are a threshold condition, not a caveat.** Both sides now treat lane readiness as a gate, not a study item.
3. **Neither option's *marginal* benefit is measured.** The bus case's 600 riders are the existing base; the marginal gain from two added buses is unquantified. The bike case's 300 rides are projected. The "observed vs. projected" asymmetry is real but weaker than the challenge claims, because the bus benefit is *plausible*, not *measured*.
4. **No cost-per-rider comparison can be made** without fabricating figures. Both sides correctly refuse to invent them.

## 2. Where the disagreement remains unresolved

| Question | Challenge position | Support position | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does the bus option's *marginal* benefit exceed the bike option's? | Assumed yes (existing base) | Unproven either way | **Unresolved — evidence gap** |
| Are older adults better served by frequency or by accessible e-bikes? | Frequency (asserted) | E-bikes address different barrier (asserted) | **Unresolved — empirical, not evidenced by either side** |
| Is "defer bikes until lanes complete" a gate or a veto? | Gate (citywide) | Veto in disguise; use corridor-level gate | **Resolved in favor of corridor-level gate** — a citywide precondition no single actor controls is functionally a "no" |
| Does reversibility/learning value favor the pilot? | Not engaged | Argued yes | **Support wins on logic** — a bounded pilot generates the missing data; bus frequency is harder to reverse and reveals less |

The honest read: the challenge wins the **burden-of-proof** argument and the **safety gate**. The support wins the **learning-value** argument and exposes a **double standard** in the challenge's equity reasoning. Neither wins the **sequencing** conclusion outright, because the marginal bus benefit is as unmeasured as the bike forecast.

## 3. Evidence gaps to close (no fabricated costs)

Collect in this order, cheapest and most decision-relevant first:

1. **Independent local bike demand measurement** — actual counts, not vendor forecast, ideally over a representative season. Set a pre-registered threshold *before* collecting (the support case suggests ~50–75 daily rides as a collapse point; the city should set its own).
2. **Mode-substitution data** — what bike trips replace (car, bus, walk, recreation). Ride counts alone don't establish congestion/climate benefit.
3. **Marginal bus ridership estimate** — how many *new* riders two added buses attract, and measured wait-time reduction. This is the missing half of the challenge's own case.
4. **Safety audit of candidate bike corridors** — lane continuity, intersection risk, crash history, severity-weighted. Gate the pilot on *corridor-level* readiness, not citywide completion.
5. **Older-adult input** — focus groups/survey on e-bike comfort, accessibility, and willingness, plus stated preference for frequency vs. e-bike access. This resolves the equity question both sides assert without evidence.
6. **True cost comparison** — per-trip costs from city procurement and operations data, once real figures exist.

## 4. Practical recommendation

**Adopt a two-track, gated decision:**

- **Fund the two additional buses now.** Rationale: the benefit is plausible, the constituency (600 riders, ~180 older adults) is identified and vulnerable, there is no unmitigated safety precondition, and the operational change is low-risk. This is a *risk-preference* call, not a proven superiority — state it as such.
- **Do not approve a citywide bike subsidy on the vendor number.** Instead, authorize a **time-boxed, corridor-gated pilot** confined to segments with continuous protected lanes, with pre-registered thresholds for ridership, substitution, safety incidents, and older-adult uptake, and a stop rule if severity-weighted incidents exceed a set level.
- **Measure the bus option's marginal ridership in parallel** so the next budget cycle compares measured-to-measured, not measured-to-projected.
- **Revisit at the pilot's end** with real numbers; if the pilot fails its thresholds, redirect to buses permanently; if it passes, the city has evidence for the next cycle.

## 5. What would change this recommendation

- **Toward bus-only:** independent counts show bike demand below threshold; substitution data show most trips replace walking/recreation; safety audit finds no corridor can achieve continuous lanes within the pilot year; older-adult input shows low willingness.
- **Toward stronger bike commitment:** validated demand holds near forecast, substitution is substantial, older-adult uptake is demonstrated, and marginal bus ridership proves small.

## 6. The one-sentence decision rule

> **Fund the bus frequency now as the lower-risk, identified-constituency option, but do not treat the vendor forecast as a basis for either approving or permanently rejecting bikes — run a lane-gated pilot to convert the projection into evidence, and hold the full-envelope decision until both options' marginal benefits are measured.**

The core judgment: the challenge's "fund buses, defer bikes" is defensible but forecloses the learning both sides say is needed; the support's "pilot instead of bus" overweights an unvalidated upside. The evidence supports **bus now + gated pilot in parallel**, with the explicit caveat that this is a risk-managed sequencing choice, not a proven ranking of the two options' total benefit.
