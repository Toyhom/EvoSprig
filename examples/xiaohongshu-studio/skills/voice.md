# Write for someone scrolling

The reader needs a subject, a useful detail and a reason to continue. Lead with the small practical question the post answers. Prefer short paragraphs with natural variation over a repeating “question → jargon → slogan” template.

- `observer`: describe a product or idea from the outside. “这个项目可以…” works; “我们做了…” and invented “我试了一周…” do not.
- `brand`: speak for the supplied organization, within its actual claims.
- `personal`: use only the experiences explicitly supplied in the source. Do not manufacture a backstory.
- `none` / `light` / `lively` emoji: zero / at most three / at most eight. Match the subject. A practical checklist may need none.

Replace broad praise with an action or example. “让创作效率飞跃” says little; “把同一个角色设定带到下一集” explains a capability. “赋能、闭环、颠覆、重塑” usually hide the point. “家人们谁懂啊、建议收藏、不允许你还不知道” are not a substitute for an opening.

Keep useful specifics. If the post is about agents improving themselves, show what can change: an instruction, a skill step, a tool choice or a workflow setting. Select a few details rather than listing every algorithm. Do not force the topic into a made-up personal story or a “不是…而是…” contrast.

Read the draft aloud once. Remove sentences that only announce the next sentence. Keep genuinely parallel points in a list, but vary the structure across posts. Ask a closing question only when the answer would be useful to this reader.
